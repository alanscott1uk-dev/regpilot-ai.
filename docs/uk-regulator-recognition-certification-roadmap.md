# RegPilot AI — independently verifiable UK regulatory recognition programme
**Decision:** pursue the strongest accurate assurance position available to a nonclinical UK business-compliance platform. Do **not** claim 'MHRA approved', 'UK government approved' or 'GxP validated' until the exact claim is supported by a competent issuer for that scope.

## Critical distinction
As of 11 November 2026, there is no general MHRA or central-government certification/approval for nonclinical compliance AI SaaS.
- MHRA's GxP data integrity guidance expects **regulated licensees** to validate computerised systems for their specific intended processes, installations, configurations and SOPs; vendor tests alone do not suffice.
- MHRA medical-device registration is explicitly **not** regulator accreditation, approval, certification or endorsement. The medical-device conformity route applies only where the software has a medical intended purpose; do not redesign RegPilot into clinical software merely to obtain a marking.
- UKAS **accredits certification bodies**, which can independently certify a provider's AI management system to ISO/IEC 42001. ISO 42001 certification is not product, regulator or clinical approval.
- Government G-Cloud 15 framework admission is **public procurement eligibility**, not a government endorsement or independent certification of each service.
- The ICO approves scheme criteria and accredited certifiers issue UK GDPR Article 42 certificates **for qualifying processing activities**; check the register to determine whether RegPilot's actual processing falls within a live scheme. ICO fee registration is not certification.

## Prioritised execution

### Gate A — define one certifiable product and nonclinical intended use
1. Freeze the intended-use statement and explicitly document clinical / SaMD exclusions and how AI-assisted decisions remain draft-only.
2. Choose first sectors: UK SME regulatory intelligence and optional pharma GMP/GDP documentation support, **not** automated batch release, clinical prescribing or original controlled GxP records.
3. Create a versioned evidence register mapping each externally assessed standard/control to implementation, test, independent reviewer, renewal date and expiry.
4. Before any regulated pilot, prove actual staging tenant isolation, trustworthy record hashes, audit exports, access reviews and restore/retention; conduct independent security testing. Clear outstanding production release gates.

### Gate B — contact the actual regulators, seeking a written position rather than endorsement
5. Send a concise technical brief to **MHRA Innovation Office** using its official enquiry form. Ask:
   - Is the described compliance research / policy review product outside medical-device qualification given no clinical purpose?
   - Which MHRA GxP data integrity, Annex 11 and licensing expectations become relevant **when a licensee uses RegPilot in a regulated process**?
   - Are there recommended external technical-assurance or validation pathways for a software supplier, without implying standalone MHRA certification?
   - Which team should assess a future regulated intended-use expansion?
6. Seek ICO innovation/sandbox input only if the proposed innovation and processing of personal data satisfy current eligibility. Regulatory sandbox support is **not** ICO approval.
7. Obtain specialist pharmaceutical software validation review by an independent RP/QA/CSV expert; arrange a controlled proof-of-concept with a suitably licensed partner. Collect independent acceptance evidence with explicit non-endorsement language.

### Gate C — recognised independent certification
8. Pursue Cyber Essentials Plus as an early technical-assurance milestone.
9. Implement and have audited an ISO/IEC 27001 ISMS for the actual service/provider scope.
10. Pursue ISO/IEC 42001 AIMS certification through a currently UKAS-accredited certification body. Ensure the certificate and audited scope identify RegPilot's AI development and operation.
11. Assess ICO UK GDPR Article 42 scheme eligibility; if none fits RegPilot's processing, do not purchase a non-approved badge and claim ICO certification.

### Gate D — public-sector route
12. Prepare a G-Cloud 15 supplier pack with actual service description, product security controls, data residency and processing, accessible user experience and pricing; monitor future admissions/reopenings.
13. Current G-Cloud 15 start date: **6 August 2026**; the published open framework describes reopening after **18 months** and **36 months**. Do not assume applications are continuously open or that listing constitutes product endorsement.
14. Publish a verified trust-and-assurance page distinguishing: externally certified; independently tested; regulatory advice received; customer-validated for particular GxP use; self-assessed; pending; not applicable. Every non-pending claim must have issuer, scope, issue date, expiry, and accessible verification reference.

## Red-line marketing phrases
**Never without legally supportable evidence:** “MHRA approved AI”, “government-approved AI”, “officially compliant”, “GxP certified by MHRA”, “registered medical device approved by MHRA”, “GDPR-certified” (without exact ICO-approved Article 42 scheme), “certified to ISO 42001” before an accredited certificate, or “government-approved because on G-Cloud”.

**Permissible after each event:** “Designed around MHRA GxP data integrity requirements”, “MHRA regulatory advice received on the stated intended use” (without endorsement implication), “Validation package independently assessed and available to regulated customers”, “[Licensee] completed customer-specific validation [under agreed confidentiality]”, “UKAS-accredited ISO/IEC 42001 certification of the AI management system [issuer/scope]”, “Cyber Essentials Plus certified [expiry]”, “Listed on G-Cloud 15 [framework/lot]”.
